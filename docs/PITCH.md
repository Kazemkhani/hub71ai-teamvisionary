# Landing Sequence — the venture case

## The problem, stated so a judge can verify it
Abu Dhabi has already won demand. New economic licences grew 29% in 2025 (ADRA). ADGM's operational entities grew 43% year on year to about 3,200 by Q3 2025. Jane Street, Brevan Howard, Man Group and Tyrus set up or expanded in ADGM. Prime office availability is 0.1%. Rents rose 14 to 32% and the emirate froze renewals in June 2026. Established UK and IB schools are waitlisted for 2026-27.

The bottleneck has moved from "will companies come" to "how fast can a company and its people become operational once they decide". Government made its own steps fast: TAMM issues a licence in about 6 minutes; Work Bundle issues a visa in about 5 days. The private steps did not move: a corporate bank account takes 2 to 6 weeks with about 30% first-time rejection, leases wait for a chequebook, school seats wait months. Hidden obligations (Emiratisation at AED 9,000 per month per unfilled role, AED 10,000 for late tax registration, visa quotas tied to office floor area) surprise mid-size movers. Nobody owns the sequence.

## Who pays and why
- Buyer: COO, CFO or head of people at a company relocating 10 to 200 roles. They lose roughly 2 to 3 months of salaries paid without operations and pay relocation agencies by the hour for sequencing knowledge.
- Channel: ADGM Registration Authority, Hub71, ADRO, the Abu Dhabi Investment Office and PRO firms, all of whom onboard these companies and currently answer sequencing questions by email.
- Price: per-relocation fee (AED 15,000 to 60,000 by headcount) plus referral revenue from banks, schools and landlords who want pre-qualified, document-complete applicants.
- Second product: the anonymised aggregate. Every plan that runs through the tool records real durations and outcomes. That is a licence-to-operational bottleneck map by zone, sector, bank and school year group that the government does not have today, and it is the evidence for the policy levers that would raise the growth rate further: shared KYC across licensing authorities and banks, direct-debit rent as the Tawtheeq default, school seat reservation windows for relocating firms, a 24-month Emiratisation glide path.

## Why this and not a checklist or a chat box
Every other entry in this hackathon, and the friend's prototype we reviewed, treats relocation as an individual's checklist. Companies move as a graph: the licence gates the visa file, the bank gates the chequebook, the chequebook gates every lease, the office floor area gates the visa quota, the school year group gates the family. The product value is in the edges, not the list. A deterministic scheduler over that graph gives a date you can plan a payroll around. AI does the work that needs language: reading the documents to build the plan, explaining each step in English and Arabic, drafting the bank cover letter and the employer rent guarantee, and translating a what-if question into plan edits.

## What world-class relocation programmes do, and what we take from each
- Singapore LifeSG, New Zealand SmartStart, UK Tell Us Once: bundle services around a life event. Moving a company is a life event; we bundle around it.
- Denmark International Citizen Service: ID number, digital ID and tax card in one visit. Our landing pass is the per-person version of that visit.
- Estonia e-Residency: fixed its banking bottleneck through fintech partners once it had the data. Our Bankable score is the data.
- Saudi RHQ programme: made the HQ decision a spreadsheet, with a 10-year Saudization exemption and procurement preference. Our obligations module makes the Abu Dhabi decision a spreadsheet.
- Singapore EDB and InvestHK aftercare teams: setup is a journey, not an event. Our product is the aftercare journey as software.

## The three-year shape
Year 1: ADGM and Hub71 companies, distribution through the licensing journey, 300 relocations, bank and school partners publishing availability into the graph. Year 2: mainland and KEZAD, outcome capture recalibrates every duration, government dashboard licensed to ADDED and ADRO. Year 3: Riyadh and Doha, the same graph with different edges.

## Honest risks
Duration data starts as medians from published ranges plus our own survey; it becomes defensible only with volume. Banks may not publish decisions; the mitigation is that applicants self-report outcomes in exchange for the free plan. Government could build this; the mitigation is that they have historically built the steps, not the sequence, and we hand them the data they lack.
